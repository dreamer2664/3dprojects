## READ THIS FIRST — BUILD AN INSTRUMENT, NOT A PIANO-SHAPED SHELL

This project is expected to become one of the most visually impressive assets in the repository. The target is not a generic black grand piano. It is an exceptional late-Victorian / early-Art-Nouveau exhibition instrument with elaborate rosewood casework, carved and gilt ornament, coloured-wood inlay, glass-and-metal filigree music desk, ornate legs and a full piano mechanism inside.

The completed object must read as an expensive, playable, meticulously maintained historical grand piano. The wood must look like real rosewood. The strings, cast frame, soundboard, bridges, action, hammers, dampers, pedals, keys, hinges, lid prop, carved sculpture, gilt mounts and inlay must be modelled with the attention normally reserved for a hero product in a high-end historical film.

**A beautiful exterior with an empty interior is a failure. A dense interior hidden under a generic shell is also a failure.** The whole asset must be excellent, including what is visible only when the lid is raised or the action is exposed.

Do not stop after a recognisable silhouette, a good first render or a successful export. Work autonomously and continue until every acceptance test is passed. Do not ask the user routine questions. Never invent measurements or technical facts; research, state assumptions and document them.

## 1. LOCK THE DESIGN TO A REAL, EXCEPTIONAL OBJECT

The main target is the **1899 Blüthner grand piano, serial number 53338**, documented by the British Antique Dealers’ Association (BADA) as the “King Edward VII, 1899, Exhibition Bluthner Grand Piano”. It is an unusually lavish rosewood piano shown at the 1900 Paris Exposition Universelle and bought by King Edward VII and Queen Alexandra in 1902 for the ballroom at Marlborough House.

The BADA record provides unusually good, specific exterior details and a large labelled image gallery. You must inspect every image, not just the overall photograph. The documented details include:

- Rosewood case with Art Nouveau inlay and Empire-style gilt mounts.
- Three tapered, four-sided baluster legs carved with sunflowers and foliage, with gilt filigree bands.
- Gilt female-head mounts with flowing hair and floral headbands atop each piano cheek.
- Cheek inlay depicting a stylised female head, whiplash lines, musical instruments and stylised foliage.
- Piano fall inlaid with coloured woods in a symmetrical Art Nouveau arrangement of foliage and whiplash lines.
- Side-panel inlay framed by Empire-inspired gilt mounts, with stars, palmettes and foliage.
- Lid inlaid with a continuous border / rinceau of interlocking stylised foliage and flowers.
- Two large ornate brass lid hinges with flowing, intertwined foliar motifs.
- Front fall ornament with bees, flowers, two winged lions, poppies, trailing ribbons and sinuous lines.
- A butterfly-shaped music desk made from bevelled glass and intricate metal filigree, with a central Tree of Life, laurel wreath and foliage.
- A specially cast, ornate, burnished gilt frame.
- A two-pedal lyre with three central spindles, gilt acanthus and palms, framed by gilt lion feet.

The BADA page lists overall length **214** and width **157**, but does not state units in the visible record. Verify the unit before using it as a hard scale value (centimetres are plausible, but do not silently assume). Establish a credible overall height, keyboard position, case depth and lid-open envelope by researching comparable late-1890s grand pianos. Do not invent a false exact dimension and claim it is documented.

### Aesthetic target

This piano should be a visually spectacular centrepiece in a 1900 European mansion music room. Use rich dark rosewood with visible natural figure, delicately coloured inlay, sculptural gilt-bronze-like ornaments, bevelled glass, intricate fretwork and an elegant open lid revealing the gold-coloured frame and complex internal stringing. It should feel expensive and historically plausible, not gaudy fantasy furniture.

**Important historical discipline:** reproduce the specific BADA object’s exterior design as closely as the available images allow. Do not add random Rococo scrolls, unrelated classical statues or modern piano features just to make the asset look more elaborate. The Art Nouveau linework and floral vocabulary must be coherent across the cheeks, fall, lid border and mounts.

## 3. BUILD THE COMPLETE GRAND PIANO

### 3.1 Outer case and cabinet construction

- Model the full rim / case as a complex laminated and curved wooden structure with believable thickness, inner and outer walls, braces, joints and edge profiles.
- Use a true grand-piano plan shape with the correct keyboard end, curved bass-side bout, tail, cheek blocks, keywell, fallboard and lid geometry.
- The lid must have real thickness, underside structure, inner veneer, edge moulding, hinges and a fitted prop stick / prop socket. Provide a convincing open-lid hero state and a closed-lid state.
- Model the fallboard, cheek blocks, key slip, keybed, nameboard, music desk, music desk pivots / supports and all visible locks and fittings.
- Recreate the three ornate legs as individual sculptural objects, including their carved sunflower / foliage motifs, baluster geometry, collars, gilt bands, foot details and casters. The carving must be deep enough to cast convincing shadows.
- Build the ornate pedal lyre with two pedals, three central spindles, gilt acanthus / palm details, and the two gilt lion-foot motifs described in the source. Include actual pedal stems and linkage to the piano interior.
- Model the butterfly-shaped music desk as a separate assembly: bevelled glass panels, metal framework, intricate filigree, central Tree of Life and laurel wreath. Show real glass thickness and physically plausible retaining hardware.
- Build all gilt mounts as separate cast-metal geometry, with attachment feet, screws / pins, undercuts, edge relief and believable casting detail.

### 3.2 Art Nouveau marquetry and decorative sculpture

This is a central part of the commission. The decorative language must follow the exact Blüthner 1899 reference; it must not look like generic ornamental decals.

- Create the female-head motifs as sculpted high-relief gilt mounts, with clean facial anatomy, flowing hair, individual locks, floral headbands and crisp silhouettes.
- Recreate the side cheek inlay with the stylised female profile, musical instruments, foliage and whiplash lines. Use separate coloured wood veneers with realistic thickness, grain and boundaries. The image must read as marquetry, not a printed graphic.
- Model the piano-fall composition with sinuous whiplash curves, poppies, leaves, trailing ribbons, bees, flowers and the winged-lion motifs. Every major motif must be individually legible.
- Create the continuous lid border as a coherent rinceau of interlocking leaves and flowers, following the curved lid perimeter accurately. Do not tile a repeating leaf strip.
- Side panels must show symmetrical, intentional inlay, gilt mounts, stars, palmettes and foliage. Keep historical ornament language consistent.
- Reproduce the ornate lid hinges as functional-looking cast brass components with layered intertwined foliate geometry, barrels, pins and screw fixings.
- All carving and raised gilt detail must cast real shadows and survive close macro renders.

Use high-resolution geometry for the female heads, winged lions, bees, poppies, floral mounts, pedal lyre and filigree. A flat normal map is not an acceptable substitute for a visibly sculptural ornament.

### 3.3 Complete piano interior — mandatory, not optional

The lid-open model must expose a richly constructed, technically plausible grand piano interior. Research the late-1890s Blüthner configuration. Do not assume every modern piano feature existed in the same form in this serial-numbered instrument.

Build and place the following, with correct relative relationships:

- Full cast-iron frame / plate with complex webbing, struts, ribs, openings, flange geometry and visible casting details. It should feel heavy and structural, not a flat gold plane.
- Structural wooden rim, braces, keybed, pinblock / wrestplank, soundboard and supporting ribs.
- Spruce soundboard with correctly oriented grain, taper / crown where appropriate, bridge(s), bridge pins and realistic bridge geometry.
- Complete string field: bass strings with copper winding where appropriate, plain steel strings, correct thickness progression, spacing, tuning-pin terminations and hitch pins. Use realistic tension lines; no floating or intersecting strings.
- Research whether this 1899 Blüthner serial 53338 uses the Blüthner Aliquot system. The Aliquot string was a fourth unstruck treble string in the upper register, but it was an option for decades. **Only include it as a historical feature if you can substantiate that it applies to this piano or if you clearly document it as a reconstruction assumption.**
- Tuning pins, agraffes / pressure bar or equivalent string termination hardware, hitch pins, plate bolts and accessible adjustment points.
- Full keyboard bed and key frame. Model each key separately, with believable key length, pivot, balance rail, front rail, balance pins, front pins, felts and bushings.
- A convincing 88-key or historically evidenced compass. Determine the actual compass through research; if no evidence exists for serial 53338, select and document a defensible late-1890s standard rather than claiming false precision.
- Complete grand action: key levers, capstans, whippen assemblies, jacks, repetition levers, hammer butts, shanks, hammer heads, backchecks, rails, flanges, pins, springs, dampers and return mechanisms. Build a dense row of individual components, not one repeated block.
- Individual felt hammer heads with visible layered felt and shaping; realistic leather/felt contact interfaces.
- Damper heads, wedges / felt, wires, lift rail and pedal connection where appropriate.
- Three pedals and their mechanical linkages consistent with the chosen historical piano, with special care not to invent a third pedal if the primary object does not have one. The BADA piano is described as two-pedal; preserve that design unless strong evidence proves otherwise.

At least one inspection render must show the action assembly clearly. If practical, create a removable action assembly or a separate exploded technical view that does not compromise the intact final piano.

### 3.4 Mechanical interaction and animation

Provide an optional animated inspection sequence:

- Lid raises and lowers smoothly on correct hinges and prop support.
- Fallboard opens and closes.
- Keys depress, hammers rise and rebound, dampers lift and return, and pedal action operates in a visually coherent way.
- At least a small representative keyboard section should demonstrate the action cycle if rigging all keys is too expensive; however, the full static action must still be modelled for all keys.
- Show the action as an exploded view if necessary to inspect the detail. Do not make parts move through the case or through other components.

A physically accurate sound simulation is not required. Mechanical plausibility and superb visual detail are required.

## 4. ROSEWOOD, VENEER, GILT METAL AND GLASS — PHOTOREAL MATERIAL WORK

### Rosewood case

- Research period rosewood and the visual properties of the species actually used by the reference. Do not casually label every brown tropical wood “Brazilian rosewood” unless supported.
- Build the case from a structural substrate with separate veneer surfaces where appropriate. Use bookmatched or matched grain on selected panels, but do not mirror every surface mechanically.
- Grain must follow the curved case perimeter, fallboard, cheek blocks, legs, lid and rails plausibly. No stretched procedural texture around the rim.
- Use real wood photographs / scans or carefully authored maps with fibre variation, pore detail and directional grain. Avoid obvious noise-generated zebra stripes.
- Use layered colour, roughness, normal, height/displacement and appropriate clear-finish maps. Main rosewood hero surfaces must use 8K textures where feasible; secondary surfaces 4K minimum.
- Build a deep, polished, age-appropriate finish with micro-scratches, slight orange-peel / finish variation only where appropriate, tiny edge wear, natural reflections and subtle handling marks. It must remain visibly wood rather than dark plastic.

### Gilt metal and cast frame

- Use a distinct material family for cast gilt mounts, polished brass fittings, burnished frame, dark iron, steel strings and piano hardware.
- Gilt ornament must have real surface relief and casting character. Use slightly varied gold tone, fine casting pores, soft tool marks, recess patina and polished high points.
- The frame should read as cast iron with a burnished gilt / gold finish consistent with the BADA description. Do not make every metal component identical gold.
- Strings must have plausible metal response and realistic gauge differences; copper wound bass strings need visible helical winding.

### Keys, felt, leather and glass

- Naturals should have plausible historic light-coloured key covering, with a historically appropriate material based on your research; sharps should be dark ebony-like material. Do not invent modern plastic.
- Model the key fronts, bevels, fine gaps, slight age variation, front pin holes / bushings and natural specular response.
- Felt should have fibre-scale shading and soft, diffuse response. Leather should show pores and restrained wear.
- Glass music desk must show bevelled edges, thickness, subtle reflections and secure fittings, while keeping the filigree readable.

## 5. GEOMETRY / TEXTURE TARGET — NO ARBITRARY CAP

This is not a game asset. There is no arbitrary polygon ceiling and no requirement to optimise for mobile hardware. Do not decimate the high-detail source mesh to satisfy a conventional real-time budget.

- **Minimum target: 40 million evaluated triangles** across the complete high-detail model, excluding cameras, lights and non-rendering helpers. Aim for **60–90 million or more** where the sculptural ornament, action, strings, cast frame, marquetry and carving justify it. There is no maximum.
- Triangle count must represent real geometry present in the master/export, not theoretical subdivision or un-applied hidden modifiers.
- The three legs, female-head mounts, winged lions, bees, flowers, pedal lyre and filigree should contain enough geometric resolution to hold up to extreme close-ups.
- Each key, string, hammer, damper, pedal, hinge, frame strut, screw, pin and decorative mount must be represented separately where physically appropriate.
- Major rosewood surfaces should have 8K textures; high-detail decoration and cast metal should have 4K–8K maps; use higher resolution if the close-up camera requires it.
- Preserve high-resolution sculpt / source geometry and full texture sources inside the project. Use preview proxies only for iteration, never as final deliverables.

If memory or render constraints occur, split modelling and rendering into isolated subassemblies, render in layers, or use linked assets. Do not quietly reduce the requested detail. Record actual technical constraints and the workaround.
