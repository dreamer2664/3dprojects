## READ THIS FIRST — THIS IS A CABINETMAKER’S MASTERPIECE, NOT AN OFFICE PROP

Create a full-size, exceptionally detailed late-Victorian English oak roll-top desk that could plausibly stand in the private study of a wealthy industrialist, barrister or estate owner around 1890–1900. It must feel heavy, meticulously constructed, warm, tactile and expensive. The timber must read as actual oak with individual boards, medullary rays, pores, end grain, joints, veneer differences and layers of old polish. The fitted interior must be a miniature world of drawers, pigeonholes, letter racks, pen trays, locks, rails and compartments.

The desk is required to function visually as a piece of furniture. The tambour is not one curved sheet. It is a true assembly of many individual slats that travels in guides and reveals the fitted interior. Drawers are not rectangular boxes glued to the front; they have sides, bottoms, backs, runners, hardware and joinery. The case has a back and sides, not a stage set front.

**Do not stop when the roll-top silhouette looks right. Do not stop when one open-state render looks attractive. Do not stop at a blockout, a procedural wood material, or a successful GLB export.** Research, model, test, render and iterate until the acceptance gate is passed. Work autonomously; do not ask the user routine questions. If a tool or process fails, investigate and recover. Do not claim a functional mechanism unless you have actually built and tested it.

## 1. THE EXACT FURNITURE TARGET — A GRAND ENGLISH DESK WITH AN EXTRAORDINARY FITTED INTERIOR

The main visual anchor is the **Victorian Oak Roll Top Pedestal Desk, circa 1890**, documented by Yola Gray Antiques through Antiques Atlas as “Roll top Desk 28”. It is an English freestanding, centre-room desk approximately **152 cm wide × 90.8 cm deep × 138 cm high**. The description and photo gallery document:

- A substantial oak, freestanding pedestal desk with pronounced solid fielded panels.
- A shaped / S-profile upper tambour section.
- A central posting slot through the tambour so correspondence can be inserted while closed.
- **33 internal drawers** in the upper fitted section.
- **15 vertical compartments / pigeonholes**.
- Lacewood drawer linings in the fitted upper interior.
- Brass label holders and brass knobs / handles on many small drawers.
- Three concealed cushion-front drawers in the central fitted area.
- Large vertical pigeonholes, letter rack, pen trays and document storage.
- Two pull-out writing-slide supports and a central modesty board.
- Two large pedestals with eight graduated drawers, a central lockable drawer and a large filing drawer.
- Sycamore drawer construction with hand-cut dovetails and adjustable dividers.
- Carved oak cup handles, cushion-front moulding, original castors and working locks.
- A tambour locking arrangement that locks the pedestal drawers when the roll-top is closed and releases them when opened.
- Rich golden oak colour and original polish / age patination.

**This is the design target.** Build a convincing interpretation of this particular grand 1890 desk, not a generic modern roll-top. Use the 33-drawer and 15-compartment arrangement as a serious complexity benchmark. Do not blindly reproduce a count if reference inspection shows a discrepancy; record the evidence and make the final layout internally coherent.

The desk should look exceptionally handsome, not overdecorated. Its beauty comes from golden / warm oak, substantial shaped sides, superb joinery, a beautifully arranged fitted interior and many small brass and wooden details. Prioritise the visual depth of the tambour, the rich oak, the interior and the satisfying sense of cabinetmaking quality.

## 4. THE TAMBOUR — IT MUST ACTUALLY BE A ROLL-TOP MECHANISM

The moving tambour is the signature element. Model it properly.

- Create individual wood slats with consistent width, slight edge profiling, realistic thickness and a carefully varied grain direction.
- Slats must be linked by a flexible backing / joint system that allows them to travel around a curved path. Research period construction and choose a plausible method; do not invent a modern plastic mechanism.
- Model the curved guides / channels inside both side cheeks. Show the thickness and profile of the tracks, the path around the upper curve and the entry into the rear storage pocket.
- The roll must be supported and constrained. It cannot float, cut through the housing, stretch, or collapse into an impossible curve.
- Include the narrow central posting slot shown in the main reference. It must pass through the slat assembly in a structurally plausible way and have clean edges and correct alignment.
- Model the tambour pull / handle, keyhole, lock escutcheon and closing stop. If the exact original latch arrangement is not visible, research a comparable period mechanism and document the chosen construction.
- Create separate open and closed positions. The roll must occupy the correct path in both states; no hidden deletion or swapping to an unrelated mesh.
- Animate a full open / close cycle with slats following the guides. Verify no intersections, gaps, visible backing tears or sudden pops in the animation.

**Acceptance standard:** the tambour should look like many individually made oak slats, each with its own edge, grain and subtle variation, forming a continuous flexible shutter. A single smoothly bent solid panel does not pass.

## 5. THE FITTED INTERIOR — MAKE THIS DESK REWARD CLOSE INSPECTION

The interior should be one of the most intricate furniture assets in the repository. The viewer should discover more detail the closer they look.

### Upper pigeonhole / drawer bank

- Reconstruct the main reference's **33 internal drawers** as individual drawer units where supported by the photographs and description. Each should have front, sides, back, bottom, runners / stops, knob or handle, label holder where fitted and a plausible internal cavity.
- Build the **15 vertical compartments** with varied but consistent widths, heights, dividers and shelves. Do not use a repeated generic grid; use a cabinetmaker's considered arrangement.
- Use lacewood for the upper drawer linings where reference-supported. Research its figure and colour so it is visually distinguishable from the main oak.
- Include the three concealed cushion-front drawers in the centre, without obvious external pulls if that is what the reference shows.
- Model brass label frames with inset cards / blank metal plates, corner radii, fixing pins, screws and aged finish. Lettering should be restrained and properly aligned.
- Add the two horizontal pen trays, letter rack, upright file compartments, small shelves and central document storage exactly where the reference photos indicate.
- Create the shaped backs, small mouldings, shelf lips, edge beads, compartment dividers and little drawer stops. Tiny details matter here.

### Writing surface and slides

- Create a full-size writing surface in real wood with correct thickness and grain direction. Include a fitted leather / baize writing area only if historically supported by the selected style; otherwise preserve the solid-oak writing top and model its wear accurately.
- If leather is used, model the inset recess, bevelled edge, border / tooling, material thickness, subtle grain and polished wear. Do not simply place a flat texture rectangle on top.
- Build both pull-out writing slides as complete assemblies with top boards, runners, guide rails, stops, underside support and realistic alignment. They must extend from their housings without clipping.
- Include minor, use-based marks on the writing area: fine paper scratches, small ink traces, localised polish variation and hand contact wear. Keep the desk well preserved, not filthy.

### Pedestal drawers and hidden structure

- Model eight graduated pedestal drawers or the reference-supported arrangement, the central lockable drawer and large filing drawer. Each drawer must have real depth and a believable box interior.
- Build dovetails where exposed, correct drawer bottoms, side runners, dust boards, stops, partition dividers and interior surfaces.
- Model the carved oak cup handles, shaped pulls, brass escutcheons, keyholes, locks, hinge plates and fixing screws individually.
- Make the bottom filing drawer larger and deeper than ordinary drawers, with the correct internal divider / file arrangement if visible.
- Model the original-style castors with metal brackets, wheel geometry, axles and fixing plates. They must support the desk and contact the floor.

## 6. LOCKING SYSTEM AND FUNCTIONAL LOGIC

The main reference describes an original working tambour lock that activates a hidden system to secure pedestal drawers when the tambour is closed and releases them when opened. Reconstruct a plausible visible mechanism.

- Model the tambour barrel lock, key, escutcheon and latch.
- Model the locking bar / rods / catches that transfer the lock state across the desk, using research and a plausible period mechanical arrangement.
- Provide a clear open / closed / locked / unlocked state demonstration.
- Drawer locks and central drawer lock should have plausible bolt / catch geometry and correctly placed keyholes.
- Do not model random gears or an impossible linkage. If the original mechanism cannot be fully resolved from the photographs, create a conservative, simplified mechanism that is physically plausible and document exactly what is inferred.
- Include an exploded technical view of the tambour and locking system, with parts separated for inspection but clearly corresponding to the assembled desk.

## 7. OAK REALISM — THE MOST IMPORTANT MATERIAL REQUIREMENT

The oak is not allowed to look like generic brown plastic, a repeated noise texture or a flat image wrapped over a box. It must look like old, selected, finished timber.

- Research quarter-sawn and rift-sawn English oak, medullary rays, pores, earlywood / latewood contrast, cathedral grain, straight grain and cut direction. Use figure appropriate to each board, not the same texture everywhere.
- Every rail, stile, panel, drawer front, slat, shelf, divider, moulding, pull and pedestal side must have deliberate grain direction.
- Where the desk uses solid fielded oak panels, model the panel construction with real thickness, frame-and-panel structure, bevels, grooves, tenons and seasonal movement gaps.
- Treat the main carcass, solid oak parts, veneers, lacewood linings and sycamore drawer parts as separate materials with appropriate grain and colour.
- Use real photographs / scanned wood references or high-quality material sources. Use procedural methods to supplement real grain, not replace it entirely.
- Create separate colour, roughness, normal, height / displacement and cavity maps. Hero timber surfaces must use 8K maps where practical; 4K is the minimum for secondary wood parts.
- Use UVs that follow the actual grain. Do not stretch one image across curved tambour cheeks or around mouldings. Each slat should have an intentional grain direction and slight individuality.
- Model end grain at exposed ends. Show pores, rays, growth-ring structure and cut marks only where they make physical sense.
- Build the old finish as layers: timber, stain, sealer / shellac or historically appropriate polish, wax and years of careful maintenance. Research the period finish. The final surface should have deep warm reflections without becoming a clear-coated plastic shell.
- Add minute, believable signs of age and use: softened edges, small dings on leading corners, faint scratches near the writing area, subtle ink residue in a few places, slightly polished areas around frequently handled knobs, fine dust in protected recesses and restrained age darkening. Do not create uniform procedural grunge.

**Macro test:** render a crop of (1) a quarter-sawn fielded panel with medullary rays, (2) five or more adjacent tambour slats, (3) a dovetailed drawer corner, (4) a lacewood-lined internal drawer and (5) a carved cup handle. Each must look like real materials and construction.

## 8. BRASS, LEATHER, GLASS AND SMALL HARDWARE

- Brass label frames, escutcheons, locks, key plates, screws, drawer pulls and slide fittings need real dimensions, backs, fixings, recesses and contact shadows.
- Brass should have controlled tarnish, fine scratches, polish on touched edges and a believable alloy hue. Avoid flat yellow or mirror gold.
- Castors should be a combination of steel / brass / darkened metal with separate wheel, axle, bracket and screw geometry.
- If a glazed panel or small glass element is supported by a reference, model actual thickness and edge highlights.
- Leather / baize should have fibre-scale detail, subtle wear, edge compression, stitching / tooling only where appropriate, and realistic roughness.
- Drawer liners should be distinct from the oak carcass. Use lacewood and sycamore only where supported by the main reference; do not invent exotic species elsewhere.
- Every screw, nail, pin, hinge, lock bolt, keyhole, handle base and stop should be correctly scaled. No floating hardware.

## 9. GEOMETRY AND TEXTURE TARGET — NO ARBITRARY LIMITS

This is a flagship high-detail furniture reconstruction, not a game-ready office prop. No arbitrary triangle cap. Do not decimate away visible joinery, slats, handles, drawer construction or fine carving to meet a conventional real-time target.

- **Minimum target: 30 million evaluated triangles** across the full high-detail master. Aim for **50–70 million or more** if justified by the separate tambour slats, fitted interior, joinery, hardware, carved pulls and high-resolution material geometry. No maximum.
- The count must reflect actual evaluated geometry present in the scene / export, not hypothetical subdivision levels or hidden modifiers that are not part of the final model.
- Every tambour slat, drawer box, divider, label holder, pull, lock element, castor, runner, moulding and visible join should be independently modelled where appropriate.
- Use real displacement / sculpted detail for the wood grain relief where macro evidence needs it. Do not simply add millions of random triangles with no visible purpose.
- Main oak surfaces: 8K textures minimum where practical. Lacewood, sycamore, leather / baize, brass and hardware: 4K–8K according to visibility and macro requirements.
- Keep high-resolution master meshes and full texture sources. Use low-resolution proxies only for viewport preview or staging.

If memory or rendering constraints arise, split the desk into logical subassemblies and render / export in sections while retaining the complete detailed master. Do not quietly lower the geometry or texture standard. Document the issue and the engineering response.
